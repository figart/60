## in and around Lviv

I also wanted to leave you with a few images of Lviv, and in particular of some of the memorable evenings passed with good friends. For those of you who have traveled Europe, you've likely encountered some of the great cities and have some understanding of the stunning architecture and historic significance embodied in the buildings in which so many Europeans live and work. Lviv rests confidently among the top tier of these urban centers. There is a sense of permanence to these streets that no American city can match.

## the final stretch

When I purchased my airfare for London, I had set aside some time for a post-mission side trip.

A little background: Thirty-five years ago, upon graduating from Texas A&M with an Economics degree, I went straight to France to have the foreign experience I'd always longed for before jumping into the traditional job scense. Mind you, I joined the Army right out of high school and spent 18 months in Schweinfurt, Germany. A few years later I spent the bulk of a summer doing a study abroad program in Santiago de Compostela. I would trade neither of those experiences for anything, but I felt both fell short of a pure immersion. I was surrounded by Americans for the majority of the time, and somehow I knew I wanted a little more.

In September of '90 I hitchiked the last 30 miles of a 5,000 mile journey from Houston to a vineyard just a few miles from Belleville, in the beautiful hills of Beaujolais. There I encountered _les vendanges_, the grape harvest, in full swing, and after a short night's rest I joined the rest of the workers—migrants, mostly—out in the fields, gently cutting the heavy, juicy bundles of grapes from the vines and dropping them into my bucket. So many stories to tell there, but I'll move on. We would be at that small vineyard another eight days before the harvest was complete. Most workers simply moved on, northward, skipping on to the next vineyard to make hay, as they say, while the sun was shining. I was broken. I was exhausted, for one, my back was sore beyond belief, and I'd honestly had enough. Were it not for the kindness of a stranger, however, a student from up north in the Haute Marne region of the country, I would have had no choice but to join the rest of the crew. I certainly didn't have an abundance of cash on me. Jean-Benoit befriended me, and suggested maybe I go back with him to his parents' house in Bologne. While he was heading back to school in Paris, he said his parents would let me stay with them while I got my bearings.

![The train from Paris to Reims]()
![Shortly after my arrival at Odile's place in Reims. We had to run down the block to buy (how French is this) a baguette.]()

What a fortuitous intervention. I spent the next three months with the Toulouse family, and worked for them, painting these massive shutters that protected some dozen windows across the front of their historic chateau. My months there were the highlight of my adventure. Combined with the experience on the vineyard and followed by a month working on a nearby dairy farm, I can't imagine a more perfect memory.

But for the past 35 years, I can count on one hand the brief encounters I've had with Jean-Benoit and his mother, Odile. And I finally had this chance to make another visit.

Odile is 81 now. Jean-Benoit is in his 50s, and is circling our planet as the pilot of an Air France jet that he always wanted to be. Roughly 25 years in the cockpit. I was lucky that he was home for a couple of the days that I stayed with Odile, and they live just across the street from one another. It felt amazing to be with them again, and I was treated like royalty.

What a way to cap off another fine adventure in Ukraine. There is, somewhere deep, a common thread between the mission of delivering rescue vehicles to a war-torn population and my reuniting with old friends who once felt like family, something beyond my obvious own privelege. I can't put words around it, unfortunately. I do know that "Ukraine 2026", as I'll refer to it, will go down as one of the most fulfilling experiences of my lifetime. I'm grateful for it, and so grateful to you for sharing it with me. À ta santé!
