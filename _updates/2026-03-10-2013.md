The end of the mission came without fanfare. A quiet and satisfying conclusion. But let me start where I left off.

## on the road

Saturday night my jet lag got the best of me, and I can't imagine I had more than a couple hours of legitimate sleep. After a nice dinner with the team I fell asleep easily, but found myself awake just after midnight. Sleep remained elusive for the rest of the night, though when we gathered in the parking lot at 5:15am the following morning I felt strong. We were on the road by 5:30 and a beautiful sunrise greeted us during our first hour. The Polish scenery was gorgeous. But as I continued to battle the glare of the slowly rising sun straight ahead, and as the landscape continued to unfold over the next few hours, I became more and more sleepy. I was sure that a short cat nap would be all I needed to recover. Unfortunately for me, that opportunity never came. Tony had decided we would go all the way into Ukraine that afternoon, and there was no time for rest stops. I'd have to do without.

I won't bore you with details of my challenges keeping my eyes open and my vehicle on the road. It was no fun, and I felt dangerous... to myself and everyone else on the road. I was thankful beyond measure when we finally made it to the Ukraine border, and our hotel would be only a short ride beyond that.

## The Polish-Ukrainian border

Crossing the border can be an exercise in patience. The Poles aren't known for their love of Ukrainians, and they seem to take pleasure in delaying humanitarian aid such as what we were delivering. But we were lucky this trip. After Tony engaged in some arguing with some of the guards, I'm sure it took scarcely more than an hour to clear all the cars, pull through the final gate and into Ukraine. We gathered at the customary spot on the side of the road just outside the border compound to take selfies with the Ukraine signage.

At the hotel, I wasted as little time as possible getting situated and laying down for a nap. It's amazing what just an hour can do to relieve the drowsiness. We soon gathered for another lovely dinner, and the team helped me celebrate my birthday, number 61.

Monday morning we gathered in the parking lot to get the ice scraped off the windshields and the vehicles warmed up for a 7am departure. On the road, we stopped at a car wash to get the vehicles cleaned up a bit, then drove on to the warehouse, our final destination. As I mentioned, there was no fanfare. We parked, unloaded some donated clothing from a couple of the vehicles, then called for a taxi. In and out.

I'm leaving Lviv in the morning, headed back to Krakow. On Thursday I fly to France to visit with an old and dear friend, and eventually make my way back to Heathrow to fly back to San Antonio on the 18th.

A heartfelt "Thank you!" to everyone who contributed. Ultimately you and I funded two vehicles, not just one. I'll likely not be traveling to Europe anytime soon to transport the second one myself, but rest assured it _will_ make it to Ukraine, thanks to Tony and his incredible team of drivers.
