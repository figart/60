Made it to Hythe yesterday, on the English coast, though not without some travel comedy — I managed to lose my flight pillow in NYC, leaving it on the plane. Then I stepped off the terminal transit bus without my backpack—my "suitcase", as it were—only to be saved by a kind fellow passenger. Then I stepped off the train at the Farringdon station in London having left my little accessory bag—charging brick, cables, water bottle, hopefully nothing of any great value—in my seat. Lesson learned: keep everything in one bag next time, and if you can't multitask (my mind was deep in a software project) then maybe just focus on keeping your shit together when on a long journey. The good news is I don't think I lost anything truly critical.

Tony, our mission leader, kindly picked me up at the Folkestone West station around 11am Thursday and got me to the hotel, where JC, my colleague from San Antonio who first introduced me to our Rotary club's work in Ukraine, met us shortly after. After Tony left, JC, Mehri and I walked the shoreline and then into Hythe and found a great little pub — the fish and chips were genuinely excellent.

After a nap I woke up with leg cramps so severe it took nearly ten minutes to get vertical, but some ibuprofen and water sorted me out. From there we gathered at Three Mariners Pub. Kevin Graham, who drove five hours from Wales and is on his fourth mission, showed up, along with Tony and a warm, energetic local named John who made quite an impression. And of course JC and Mehri, with whom I walked from the hotel.

I had a lovely, leisurely English breakfast this morning, a walk about the beautiful town, and felt the growing anticipation of the mission, which has now begun.

Tony picked me up at 2:35pm local time and took me to my "new" car, a Nissan X-Trail. She and I will become well acquainted over the next 3 days. I met a couple other drivers, and the four of us made the 20-minute ride up the coast to the docks, where we sit now. A few other drivers will meet us here. This was my second time to drive a vehicle from the right front seat, and my first time to drive on the left side of the road. Not quite as stressful as I'd anticipated.

![Tony arrives to take me to my vehicle](/assets/images/updates/2026-03-06-1054/photo-6.webp)

We'll drive onto the boats here in just a few minutes. By 9pm we should be landing in Dunkirk. If you haven't seen the movie, I recommend it.
