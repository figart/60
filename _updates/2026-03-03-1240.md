If you're reading this, you either donated to [a cause I am passionate about](https://www.rotarysa.org/initiatives/ukraine-relief), or you have encouraged or contributed (in one way or another) to my inspiration to undertake [this adventure](/2026/01/28/why-im-going-back-to-ukraine.html). Thanks for the support, in particular the dollars, which translate directly to lives saved.

<aside class="callout" markdown="1">
From a [newsletter](/assets/images/updates/2026-03-03-1240/RCSA-ICS-Update-10-Covering-Dec-2025-Feb-2026.pdf){:target="_blank"} that my Rotary club is circulating internally:

On average, a full-sized ambulance will carry 37 wounded civilians and soldiers a month from aid tents to fixed/hardened hospitals. They work mostly behind the front lines out of reach of most drone attacks.

The converted 4x4 patient retrieval vehicles will carry upwards of 60 people a month. They operate in the front lines and often come under direct attack. They are workhorses and take a lot of punishment. And they are truly life-savers.

</aside>

My bag is packed, I'm ready to go. I'll be boarding the plane tomorrow around lunch time and landing at Heathrow Thursday morning, local time. I'll have one night there, hanging out at the pub with the other convoy drivers that evening. If I recall correctly, we'll catch the ferry across the Channel the following night (Friday), then drive all night until sometime Saturday afternoon before stopping to rest.

I'll share a few more updates like this one. I'll send a brief email here and there, possibly including a link to a more elaborate update and hopefully a photo or two. No pressure to read them, these are busy times for many of us. We're only talking about roughly a week until I leave Ukraine so I'll go quiet before you know it.
